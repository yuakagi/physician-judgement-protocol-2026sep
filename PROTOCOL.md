# Blinded discrimination study: protocol and analysis plan

**Research question.** Whether, and at what rate, clinicians can distinguish a patient's real
clinical course from a course generated by the Watcher simulator.

**Version 1.0, fixed on 24 September 2026, before any rating was collected.**
Ethics: Research Ethics Committee of the University of Tokyo, 2023367NI.

The sampling, rater eligibility, rating period, instrument, primary measure, decision rule and
secondary analyses specified below were fixed before the first rating. Any subsequent change is
recorded in `CHANGELOG.md` with its date, and any analysis added subsequently is reported as
exploratory. The document is structured after the elements of the OSF preregistration template [1].

---

## 1. Purpose

This study examines whether clinicians presented with a patient's real clinical course and a
generated course side by side can identify the real one, and at what rate. It complements the
quantitative evaluation of the simulator in the accompanying manuscript. That evaluation compares
distributions and sequential structure between generated and real timelines but does not test
whether generated courses are distinguishable to a clinician reading them. Cases are drawn at
random from the simulation output, and the rate of correct identification is estimated under a
pre-specified decision rule.

**Hypothesis.** The null hypothesis is that the proportion of correct identifications equals
one half, that is, that clinicians cannot distinguish the generated course from the real one. The
study is designed to bound this proportion within 0.40 to 0.60 if the null hypothesis holds, and
to detect a proportion of 0.60 or greater if it does not (section 7).

---

## 2. Design

Each case presents one patient's recorded history up to an evaluation time, followed by two
continuations over the same 7-day window: the real continuation and one generated from the same
history. The two are labelled A and B in random order, and the rater indicates which is real.
The design is a two-alternative forced choice (2AFC).

2AFC was chosen because its chance level is one half by construction and the proportion correct
does not depend on a response criterion [2, 3].

---

## 3. Cases

### Source

Cases are drawn from the Monte Carlo simulation output generated as described in the Methods of
the accompanying manuscript: 256 trajectories per evaluation point for patients of the held-out
test split. No additional generation is performed for this study.

### Eligibility

A patient is eligible when all of the following conditions hold. The sampling code applies
these conditions.

|  | Criterion |
| --- | --- |
| C1 | Belongs to the held-out test split |
| C2 | Has at least one admission with at least one simulated evaluation day |
| C3 | The admission record (admission time, discharge time, admitting department) is readable |
| C4 | For the evaluation day drawn, at least one generated trajectory exists |
| C5 | Both the real and the generated continuation contain at least one record in the 168 hours after the evaluation time |

A patient failing C4 or C5 after the draw is excluded without replacement, and the realised
counts are reported.

### Sampling

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| Unit | One case per patient |
| Admission | One of the patient's eligible admissions, selected at random |
| Evaluation day | One of the simulated evaluation days of that admission, selected at random; evaluation is at 12:00 |
| Generated arm | One of the 256 trajectories for that evaluation point, selected at random |
| Window | 168 hours after the evaluation time, the window used in the manuscript's main analyses |
| History shown | The 7 days preceding the evaluation time |
| Randomisation | A single recorded seed governs every draw (Appendix B) |

Using one case per patient makes the cases independent observations. Evaluation points within
the same admission are not independent.

### Strata

Cases are drawn in proportion to the strata below, so that the composition of admissions in the
sample reflects that of the cohort rather than sampling variation. Equal allocation was not used
because it would over-represent rare kinds of admission.

| Stratum | Levels |
| --- | --- |
| Length of stay | < 7 days / 7 to 29 days / 30 days or more |
| Admitting department | The departmental code of the admission; the six specialty groups shown to raters (medical, surgical, paediatrics, obstetrics and gynaecology, emergency, other) are aggregates of these codes |
| Circulatory support | Whether a catecholamine (ATC C01CA) was ordered as a prescription or injection between admission and discharge |

Strata are defined by the cross-classification of the three variables. Circulatory support is the
marker of critical illness that these data support, as the record contains neither vital signs
nor procedures. Timeline length and complexity are not used as strata, because stratifying on
them would over-represent long and event-dense courses.

### Numbers

Each rater receives **90 cases**: 15 common to all raters and 75 unique to that rater.

```
6 raters   540 judgements on 465 distinct patients
5 raters   450 judgements on 390 distinct patients
4 raters   360 judgements on 315 distinct patients
3 raters   270 judgements on 240 distinct patients
```

The case set is constructed once, before any rating, with a recorded seed (Appendix B). It
contains 15 common cases and six blocks of 75 unique cases, one block per rater, allowing up to
six raters.
Blocks are assigned in the order in which raters first log in. Any unassigned block is not
judged. The realised composition of the set is recorded in `CHANGELOG.md`.

Predominantly unique cases are used because judgements of the same case by different raters are
correlated (cases differ in difficulty), which would leave the effective sample size undetermined
in advance. Using predominantly unique cases limits this dependence. The 15 common cases are
included in the primary analysis and also used to describe agreement between raters.

## 4. Instrument

|  | Question | Scale | Required |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Which continuation is the real one | A / B | Yes |
| 2 | Confidence | sure / probably | Yes |
| 3 | Basis for the judgement | Free text | No |
| 4 | Whether the patient is recognised | Yes / No | No |

Questions 1 and 2 are answered together by selecting one of four options ("A, sure",
"A, probably", "B, probably", "B, sure"). No "cannot tell" option is offered; the forced choice
is what fixes the chance level at one half. No plausibility rating is collected, as it would
measure a different and less well-defined quantity and would require anchoring and reliability
assessment of its own.

Question 3 records the features raters relied on, which is the most informative output should
the arms prove distinguishable. Question 4 records recognition of the patient, which cannot be
reversed and is reported as a limit on independence.

The interface is available in Japanese and English, generated from a single table of strings.
The wording is reproduced in Appendix A.

## 5. Raters

### Eligibility

|  | Inclusion |
| --- | --- |
| R1 | Licensed physician |
| R2 | Co-author of the accompanying manuscript. The ethics approval covers the research team; clinicians outside it would require a separate application. This is stated as a limitation |
| R3 | Took no part in building the simulator's evaluation pipeline, the rating system or the case set, and saw no case before receiving credentials |
| R4 | Has no access to the rating server, its database or the simulation output |
| R5 | Agrees to the rating instructions (section 6) before starting |

|  | Exclusion |
| --- | --- |
| X1 | The author who built the rating system and performs the analysis |
| X2 | Any person who has seen the ground truth of any case |
| X3 | Any person who discussed the content of a case with another rater before the close of the rating period; if this becomes known, that session is excluded and the fact reported |

### Recruitment and rating period

All co-authors meeting R1 to R4 are invited with this protocol and a statement of the case load
(90 cases). Participation is voluntary, and no reply is requested. The raters are those who log in
and rate.

Credentials are issued from 24 September 2026. **The rating period closes at 23:59 JST on
5 October 2026.** Raters may interrupt and resume at will. Answers are saved as entered. A rater
completes participation by pressing "Submit", after which no answer can be changed. Reminders may
be sent. They carry no information about any case or about other raters' progress.

### Inclusion in the analysis

| Level | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Rater | Included when the session was submitted through the interface by the close of the rating period. A session not submitted by then is excluded in full; its number of answered cases is reported |
| Judgement | Every answered case of an included session. Unanswered cases are excluded and counted per rater. No judgement is excluded from the primary analysis for recognition of the patient or for any other reason; such exclusions are confined to the sensitivity analyses (section 8) |
| Case | Included when at least one included rater answered it |

The number of raters is therefore not fixed in advance. It is determined by voluntary
participation within the rating period. The analysis is performed and reported for that number,
with the interval it supports (section 7).

### Safeguards, given that the raters are co-authors

- The analyst does not rate (X1). The analyst's account holds only a preview session, flagged in
  the database and excluded from every progress page and export.
- The interface never reveals which continuation is real, to any user.
- Every administrative page view and every export is logged.
- The export is made once, after the close of the rating period, and analysed as specified in
  sections 7 and 8. Progress pages display counts only.

## 6. Blinding and rating instructions

### Measures implemented in the system

| Measure |
| --- |
| The ground-truth label is held on the server and is never transmitted to the browser |
| The side on which each continuation appears is randomised per rater and then fixed |
| Both continuations are rendered by the same code from the same six fields (day, time, type, code, text, result) |
| Patient and trajectory identifiers and calendar dates are removed; days are counted from the evaluation day, and age is shown in whole years |
| Nothing is aggregated, de-duplicated or filtered. Whatever differs between the arms is what the study measures |
| No feedback is given at any point |
| Times are shown to the minute in both arms, as recorded or generated |
| Codes (ICD-10, ATC, laboratory) are shown on hover. Their format is identical in both arms |

### Instructions to raters

1. Judge from the information on the screen only. Do not consult the hospital record or any
   other source.
2. Do not discuss the content of any case with anyone, including other raters, until the rating
   period has closed.
3. Answer every case if possible. If you cannot finish, submit what you have completed before the
   deadline; unanswered cases are reported, not penalised.
4. Tick "I recognise this patient" whenever you believe you do. The case remains in the set.

## 7. Primary measure and decision rule

**Primary measure**: the proportion of judgements in which the rater identified the real
continuation, pooled over included raters, with a 95% confidence interval from a case-level
cluster bootstrap (1,000 replicates, percentile interval, recorded seed). The cluster is the
case, because judgements of a common case by different raters are not independent.
Each replicate samples the included cases uniformly with replacement, drawing the same number
of cases as in the analysis set. All included judgements of each sampled case are retained,
including repeated copies when a case is drawn more than once. Accuracy is the total number of
correct judgements divided by the total number of judgements in that replicate. The interval
uses the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles with linear interpolation.

**Decision rule**: the result is described as lying within the pre-specified equivalence bounds
only if the entire confidence interval lies within **0.40 to 0.60**. The interval, rather than
the verdict, is reported.

### Power, by number of included raters

Power is estimated by simulation using the primary analysis above, with every rater answering
all 90 cases. Cases are independent, and all raters have the stated marginal accuracy. Each
cell gives two estimates: independent judgements within common cases / perfectly concordant
judgements within common cases. The latter assumes that all raters are correct or all are
incorrect on a given common case. These are planning scenarios, not bounds on actual power.

Each scenario uses 10,000 simulated studies and 1,000 bootstrap replicates per study (seed
20260925). Power is the proportion of intervals meeting the row's criterion; the Monte Carlo
standard error is at most 0.5 percentage points. The calculation preserves the greater weight
of common cases in pooled accuracy. It uses synthetic outcomes only and is reproduced by
`python power_simulation.py` (NumPy 2.4.6).

|  | 6 raters, 465 cases | 5 raters, 390 | 4 raters, 315 | 3 raters, 240 | 2 raters, 165 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Detect discrimination at 0.60 (interval excludes 0.5) | 99.6% / 93.3% | 99.1% / 89.7% | 96.5% / 88.0% | 89.9% / 80.5% | 76.4% / 69.7% |
| Conclude accuracy lies within the pre-specified equivalence bounds when accuracy is 0.5 (interval within 0.40 to 0.60) | 99.4% / 87.5% | 97.7% / 83.0% | 93.8% / 74.9% | 82.2% / 63.0% | 54.2% / 40.8% |
| Same, within 0.425 to 0.575 (reported, not the rule) | 87.1% / 47.4% | 78.4% / 39.6% | 62.7% / 29.1% | 39.0% / 14.8% | 5.8% / 0.2% |

These calculations do not establish adequate power to exclude a small effect such as accuracy
of 0.55. This is a limitation of the study and is stated as such.

### Raters are treated as fixed

With so few raters (at most six), the between-rater variance cannot be estimated. Inference is
therefore conditional on the raters included and is not extrapolated to clinicians in general.
Each rater's accuracy is reported alongside the pooled value.

## 8. Secondary and sensitivity analyses

All analyses below are pre-specified.

|  | Analysis | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| a | Accuracy per rater (90 cases; interval half-width approximately 0.10) | Consistency between raters |
| b | Accuracy by confidence (sure vs probably); proportion of "sure" answers that were incorrect | Describe the relationship between confidence and accuracy; confidence alone establishes neither clinical realism nor whether the material was read |
| c | Agreement on the 15 common cases (Fleiss' kappa) | Exploratory; the interval is wide with 15 cases |
| d | Proportion of cases in which the patient was recognised; accuracy with those cases removed | Limit on independence |
| e | Accuracy restricted to sessions in which all 90 cases were answered | Sensitivity only |
| f | Accuracy including the answered cases of sessions not submitted by the deadline | Exploratory; renders excluded material visible |

For analysis c, A/B responses are mapped to the continuation selected as real (real or
generated), so that categories are consistent across raters despite randomised display order.
Fleiss' kappa is calculated over included raters using only common cases answered by every
included rater. Cases with any missing answer are excluded from this analysis only, without
imputation; the numbers of raters and retained and excluded common cases are reported.

A 95% percentile interval for kappa is obtained by resampling the retained common cases with
replacement (1,000 replicates, recorded seed), retaining all raters' responses together and
using the same number of cases in each replicate. Percentiles use linear interpolation. Kappa
is reported as not estimable if fewer than two raters or two complete common cases remain, or
if the expected agreement is one. If any bootstrap replicate has undefined kappa, the interval
is reported as not estimable and the number of undefined replicates is reported; undefined
replicates are not discarded to construct an interval. These restrictions do not change the
primary analysis.

## 9. Scope of the conclusions

- A discrimination rate near chance does not by itself establish clinical realism; raters may
  fail because the material is realistic or because it is difficult to read. The confidence split
  (b) and the free-text basis bear on this distinction.
- The study is not equivalent to an assessment by independent clinicians. Independent evaluation
  remains future work requiring separate ethics approval.
- Inference does not extend beyond the raters included.
- The number of raters reflects voluntary participation within the rating period rather than a
  predetermined target; the power table (section 7) governs the interpretation of the interval.

## 10. Data and records

- Responses are saved as entered and remain editable until submission, which is explicit and
  confirmed.
- All processing takes place on an institutional server within the hospital network. No patient
  data leave the institution.
- Exported once, after the close of the rating period: choice, ground truth, correctness,
  confidence, recognition, free text, submission time, and the provenance of each case (patient,
  admission, evaluation day, trajectory index).
- The analyses of sections 7 and 8 will be implemented in code released with the manuscript.
- Any departure from this protocol is recorded in `CHANGELOG.md` with its date and a statement of
  whether results had been seen.

## Appendix B. Reproducing the sample

```sh
python manage.py build_rating_cases \
    --name round2 --source-dir <simulation output> --seed 20260924 \
    --n-core 15 --n-unique-per-rater 75 --n-raters 6 --horizon-hours 168
```

The seed and source directory are stored with the case set. Allocation to strata is proportional
with largest remainders; selection within strata, the evaluation day and the generated trajectory
are drawn from the same seeded generator. The order in which a rater encounters the cases and the
side on which the real continuation appears are drawn from a generator seeded by the case-set
seed and the rater, so that they are fixed at first login and reproducible.
